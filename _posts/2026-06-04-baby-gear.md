When I was pregnant with my first, I spent an embarrassing number of hours researching baby gear. I had multiple spreadsheets. I read Reddit threads at 2am. I doom scrolled TikTok. I compared things that, in retrospect, did not need to be compared.

Now that we're expecting our second, I've gone back through all of it — what we actually used, what collected dust, and what I'd do differently. This is that list.

A few things to know before we dive in: my first was a winter baby, I breastfed and pumped for 11 months (including a stint of triple feeding, which is exactly as exhausting as it sounds), and we travel more than the average family. That context shapes a lot of these picks.

## Feeding

**Bibs — [KeaBabies Bib][https://amzn.to/43fmzXx]**
Honest note: we started with the Lalo bib and it wasn't great at catching food. For our second we're switching to KeaBabies. Sometimes round two is the real review.

**Bottles — [Dr. Brown's Plastic Bottles][https://amzn.to/4mFAlLY]**
These worked well for us, but bottles are deeply personal to your baby. I'd genuinely recommend the [Babylist bottle sample set][https://www.babylist.com/gp/babylist-bottle-box-5-bottles-1764018277/74195/2473615] before committing — it lets you try a handful before buying a full set. For baby #2 we're switching to glass.

**Burp Cloths — [Yoofoss Muslin Burp Cloths][https://amzn.to/4u65xqq]**
Buy more than you think you need. We had stacks everywhere — the diaper bag, the car, next to every chair in the house. Muslin is the move.

**High Chair — [Tripp Trapp with Newborn Set][https://amzn.to/4voX3es]**
We genuinely loved the newborn set, which let us use it from very early on. The one caveat: it's not portable. Ours lived in the kitchen and stayed there. If you need something you can move around or take to a grandparent's house, this isn't it — but as a permanent seat it's excellent.

**Bottle Drying Rack — [OXO Tot Drying Rack][https://amzn.to/4vIAmTn]**
Compact, no complaints. Does exactly what it should.

**Travel Bottle Drying Rack — [Ubbi Travel Rack][https://amzn.to/4emtR1V]**
We tried the [OXO travel drying rack][https://amzn.to/4mIkQ5O] and it just didn't dry bottles well, water would pool... we survived but we're swapping it out for the Ubbi travel rack for baby #2.

**Bottle Cooler — [Ceres Chill][https://amzn.to/4tsOPl5]**
This was one of my most-used items as a pumping mom. It keeps milk cold for hours, works as a travel cooler, and I used it constantly on the go. If you're planning to pump at all, I'd add this to your list.

**Bottle Sterilizer — [Papablic Sterilizer & Dryer][https://amzn.to/48bckXk]**
This did the job for baby #1. For our second we're upgrading to the [Momcozy Bottle Washer and Sterilizer][https://amzn.to/4vwt58A], which came recommended by several families we trust. I'll report back.

**Breast Pump (Wearable) — [Willow Go][https://amzn.to/4sRC7Lj]**
I pumped for 11 months and the Willow Go was what made that possible. It's hands-free, quiet enough to wear in a meeting, and once my supply was established it became my primary pump. It's frequently on sale and is FSA/HSA eligible — worth checking if insurance will subsidize it before you buy.

**Breast Pump (Traditional) — [Spectra S1][https://amzn.to/4u1FvEA]**
I used this in the early weeks when supply was still coming in and the Willow wasn't as effective. It's a workhorse and a good backup to have. Also FSA/HSA eligible.

**Haakaa Ladybug — [Haakaa Ladybug][https://amzn.to/4eBgDPr]**
My lactation consultant recommended this over the suction Haakaa specifically because we were triple feeding. If you're in a similar situation — or just want a low-effort way to collect letdown — this is the one to get.

**Silverettes — [Silverettes][https://amzn.to/4sJjQ2L]**
I don't say this lightly: these were a lifesaver in the early weeks of breastfeeding. Worth every penny.

**Solid Feeding Utensils — [DRKIO Fork & Spoon Set][https://amzn.to/4tAKMmH]**
Simple, baby-friendly, and they held up well through the messy phase.

## Baby Gear

Our pick for an infant seat. Lightweight, easy to click in and out, and compatible with the Nuna MIXX stroller below — which made our lives much easier those first months.

**Stroller — [Nuna MIXX Next][https://www.bloomingdales.com/shop/product/nuna-mixx-next-stroller?ID=3946391]**
Our everyday stroller. It handled sidewalks, uneven terrain, and a lot of daily miles. The car seat compatibility was a big selling point for us.

If you travel, a compact stroller is non-negotiable. The Silver Cross Jet folds small enough to fit in an overhead bin and is significantly easier to manage in airports than a full-size stroller.

The budget-friendly travel car seat that actually works. We didn't want to haul our Nuna on every trip, and this has been a perfectly good solution. The one thing is the harness straps are finicky/hard to adjust but since we only use it for travel we're okay with it not being perfect.

**Travel Crib — [Guava Lotus Travel Crib][https://amzn.to/42kq3r9]**
Easy to set up, packs down well, and our daughter slept great in it. A solid pick if your family travels frequently.

**Car Mirror — [Car Mirror][https://amzn.to/4mJUSz8]**
Inexpensive and genuinely useful in those early months when you want to glance back and make sure they're okay. Didn't feel like we needed the camera one and have no regrets.

## Nursery

**Crib — [Babyletto Gelato][https://amzn.to/3OwmYkK]**
This was a gift, and it worked beautifully. One thing worth noting: we ended up transitioning our daughter straight from this to a twin bed (timing worked out that way), so we never actually used the toddler bed conversion — but it's there if you need it.

**Glider — [Babyletto Kiwi][https://amzn.to/4tsObnF]**
I spent a lot of time in this chair those first months. It's comfortable, it looks good, and it holds up. No regrets.

**Baby Monitor — [Infant Optics][https://amzn.to/4cIMCL9]**
We specifically wanted something not connected to wifi, and this fit the bill. Two years in, the battery life is starting to fade — but we also barely use it anymore, so it's done its job.

**Sound Machine — [Hatch Rest+][https://amzn.to/4cXvrXl]**
We used this from the start and still use it for our toddler. The app control is genuinely useful when you don't want to open the door and wake them up. We've already gotten another for our second that is on the way. 

**Room Thermometer — [Govee][https://amzn.to/4tsuxYU]**
Small, cheap, and gives you one less thing to wonder about at 3am.

**Dresser Dividers — [OXO Dresser Divider][https://amzn.to/4cr77gA]**
Tiny baby clothes are chaos without these. Highly recommend. I also got the cheap amazon ones and truly regret it, they just didn't work well at all. I would adjust them every few months as the baby clothes sizes grew.

Affordable, does the job, doesn't need to be complicated.

## Diapering

**Diapers — Huggies**
We tried a few brands and landed on Huggies. That said, diaper preferences are very baby-specific — what works for one kid may not work for another.

**Changing Pad — [Keekaroo][https://amzn.to/4vNTDDa]**
No covers to wash, easy to wipe down. It's pricier than alternatives, but if budget allows it's a nice upgrade. The [SkipHop][https://amzn.to/3QRVzKE] and [Papablic][https://amzn.to/3OWwQ7r] options are solid budget alternatives.

**Diaper Pail — [Ubbi Pail][https://amzn.to/4tpL1AP]**
Works well and doesn't smell. Pro tip from experience: get one for each floor of your home. Trust me! For our downstairs we had diapers, a travel diaper pad along with the diaper pail and it was so nice to have that secondary changing station. 

**Wipes — Costco**
We tried the name brands. We came back to Costco. Bulk wipes is just the right move.

That's the full list of what we actually used and would buy again. You'll figure out what matters for your family — no spreadsheet required.
